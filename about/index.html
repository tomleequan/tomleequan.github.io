Manufacturing
Our own production facilities and in-house R&D — RF hardware is developed and assembled in-house rather than bought in and re-badged, which is what keeps the datasheet and the delivered unit in step.

What happens between the order and the carton

The routine every unit goes through before it leaves the building:

01

Assembly and spot check

Components are placed automatically, then the assembly is spot-checked by hand — mainboard, case, panel and finish are all part of the check.

02

Core component review

The core components are checked and re-checked against the datasheet for omissions and errors before a unit is allowed to move on.

03

72-hour test

RF technicians run each unit for 72 hours — the point is to catch the fault that only appears after a day of running, not the one that appears on the bench.

04

Packing and loading

Units are packed for transport, the quantities and the buyer's address are checked, and the fastest available collection is booked.

How an enquiry runs

01

Send the requirement

Channel count, input sources, output standard, rack space and the market you deliver to. A photo of the rack or a channel list is enough to start.

02

Model list and channel plan

We come back with the specific models, how they connect, and what each one does in the chain — not a generic brochure list.

03

Confirm against the datasheet

Every parameter on the model pages comes from the manufacturer datasheet shipped with that model. If a detail is not in it, we ask the factory rather than guess.

04

Order, shipping and support

Quotation, payment terms and freight options are confirmed in writing, with technical support on the models supplied.
