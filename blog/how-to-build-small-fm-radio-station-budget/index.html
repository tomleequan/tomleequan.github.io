Start a Small FM Station: The Realistic Path

Launching a low-power FM radio station is more achievable than most people think — and cheaper than ever with modern solid-state transmitters. Here is the full picture.

Step 1: Licensing & Legal Basics

Before buying anything, check your country's regulator:

  • Low-power license — many countries offer simplified licenses for <100W stations
  • Frequency coordination — your frequency must not interfere with existing stations
  • Part 15 / exempt class — in some regions, very low power (0.5–10W) needs no license

Rule: understand licensing *before* you spend money on equipment.

Step 4: Antenna & Feeder

The antenna is 50% of your coverage:

  • Dipole antenna — simplest, good for low power
  • Multi-bay antenna — for higher gain and better range
  • Ground plane (GP) antenna — omnidirectional, easy install

Don't forget:

  • Low-loss coax cable (RG213 or LMR-400 for longer runs)
  • Lightning protection and grounding
  • Mast or tower (20–50 m dramatically improves range)

Antenna + feeder + mast: $300–2,000

Step 5: STL / Streaming Link

If the studio is away from the transmitter site, you need a link:

  • STL over IP — stream audio over the internet (cheapest)
  • Wireless STL — dedicated microwave link (5.8 GHz)
  • FM to FM rebroadcast — relay another station's signal

STL budget: $200–1,500

Going On Air: Launch Checklist

1. ✅ License approved (or confirmed exempt)

2. ✅ Frequency checked for interference

3. ✅ Transmitter tested with dummy load first

4. ✅ SWR tuned below 1.5:1

5. ✅ Audio processing (compressor/limiter) configured

6. ✅ RDS shows your station name

7. ✅ Backup: automation software runs 24/7

8. ✅ Logging system (required in many countries)

Bottom line: a small FM station is achievable for $2,000–5,500. Spend the most on the antenna system and audio processing — they determine the professional sound that keeps listeners coming back.
