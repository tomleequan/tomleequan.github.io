The Antenna Is Half Your Station

A perfect transmitter feeding the wrong antenna delivers a fraction of its potential coverage. Antenna choice determines where your signal goes and how well it gets there.

First: Know Your Band

BandFrequencyTypical use
LF/MF (AM)530–1700 kHzAM broadcast (tall towers)
HF/SW (shortwave)3–30 MHzInternational broadcast
FM (VHF)87–108 MHzFM radio
VHF TV167–223 MHz (Band III)TV broadcast
UHF TV470–862 MHzTV broadcast, DVB-T

Antenna Types by Use Case

FM Broadcast Antennas

  • Single dipole — simple, low power, omnidirectional-ish
  • Multi-bay dipole (2/4/6/8 bay) — higher gain, stacked vertically for more coverage
  • Panel antenna — flat panel with high gain, directional patterns
  • Circular polarized — better for FM reception in vehicles

Rule of thumb: every time you double the number of bays, you roughly add 3 dB gain (≈40% more effective radiated power).

VHF / UHF TV Antennas

  • Panel antennas — common for TV stations; stackable for gain
  • Slot antennas — omnidirectional pattern from a cylindrical radiator
  • Yagi antennas — directional, high gain, used for STL links and reception
  • Batwing (superturnstile) — classic TV broadcasting antenna

AM / Shortwave Antennas

  • Vertical tower (monopole) — standard AM broadcast radiator
  • Tuning unit (ATU) — matches transmitter impedance to the tower
  • Curtain arrays — high-gain shortwave arrays for international service
  • Biconical — broadband, up to 50kW input

Gain, Polarization and Pattern

  • Gain (dBd/dBi): higher gain concentrates the signal toward the horizon — good for coverage distance
  • Polarization: FM radio is usually circular or horizontal; TV often horizontal; AM is vertical
  • Radiation pattern: omnidirectional covers all directions; directional focuses on a service area

Matching the Antenna to Your Setup

1. Frequency: the antenna must be cut for your exact channel/frequency

2. Power rating: must exceed your transmitter power (safety margin recommended)

3. Impedance: standard is 50Ω — use an ATU if the match is poor

4. Feeder cable: low-loss coax or rigid line for long runs

5. Height: the single biggest coverage factor after power

Practical Checklist Before Buying

  • [ ] Frequency band and exact frequency
  • [ ] Transmitter power (kW rating)
  • [ ] Coverage pattern needed (omni vs directional)
  • [ ] Antenna mounting (tower type, wind load)
  • [ ] Connector type and feeder cable length
  • [ ] Polarization requirement
Bottom line: buy the antenna *after* you know your frequency, power and coverage pattern. A matched antenna system delivers dramatically better coverage than a more powerful transmitter with the wrong antenna.
